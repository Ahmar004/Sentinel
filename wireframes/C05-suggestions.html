  1. Option 1 shows every safeguard passing individually, not a single "safe" chip. Five named safeguards, each with its own PASS, because a coordinator confirming a route should be able to see which specific checks were run, not just trust a summary verdict. FR7.3
  2. Option 3 renders rejected in the same list, not filtered out. The failing safeguard and the exact cell are named in place, and a rejected card carries no Confirm button at all - there is nothing on it that could be mis-tapped into an action. FR7.4
  3. Mobile keeps the same three ranks, the same safeguard vocabulary and the same rejection, only the card copy is shortened; nothing that changes the decision is cut for width. FR7.1 FR7.3
  4. When every candidate fails, the surface says so in words and offers nothing to confirm. Three rejected cards each name a distinct failing safeguard, proving the rejections are per-route and not a single blanket refusal, and the closing line states plainly that no safe option exists for this alert right now. FR7.5
  5. The no-safe-option statement is styled with the critical accent, not the neutral one, because it is telling the coordinator that dispersal cannot be safely suggested, which is itself a fact worth the same visual weight as a critical cell. FR7.5
  6. Template phrasing keeps the identical structure, ranks and safeguard results as the model phrasing above; only the sentences read mechanically. The badge changes from MODEL to TEMPLATE and a banner states the model is unavailable, so a coordinator is never left guessing why the wording feels different. FR7.6 NFR6
  7. Confirmed shows who and when, the outcome countdown, and the actuation boundary in the same breath. "Confirmed by a.rahman at 14:03:05", "PENDING - 9m 53s remaining", and the sentence that nothing was actuated all sit on one card, because outcome tracking and the advisory boundary are two halves of the same guarantee: Sentinel recorded a decision, and it did not act on it. FR7.7 FR8.6
  8. The superseded option reads EXPIRED, not deleted. Once one option is confirmed the others stop being offerable, and the history of what was proposed is preserved rather than removed, which is what makes the audit trail in FR7.8 possible. FR7.8
