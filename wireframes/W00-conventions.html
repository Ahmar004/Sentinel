1. What is greybox and what is not

Everything in these wireframes is grey: layout, type, controls, charts. One thing is not. The four observation states and the four risk bands render in their real colour, fill pattern and label, because those eight treatments are the requirement itself (NFR5) and the honesty invariant cannot be reviewed if they are greyed out with everything else. A reviewer should be able to argue about the eight treatments here and about nothing else.

Type is the system UI stack. No web font is loaded, no CDN is contacted and no request leaves the machine, which is the same no-paid-service rule the product itself is built under. Touch targets are 44px. Spacing is a 4px scale.

2. Three channels, three dimensions

A cell carries three independent facts at once and each gets its own visual channel, so none overwrites another. This resolves the ambiguity in design.md, which said risk band draws as "cell outline or fill" while density shading also fills. Both cannot own the fill.

Because the three channels are independent, a cell can read as "dense, observed, and only in the watch band" in one glance. A viewer who cannot see colour still separates all eight, because pattern and outline weight carry the distinction on their own, and every cell also carries a text label (NFR5).

3. The four observation states

A cell carries an observation state always. It carries a risk band only when observed. These are two separate fields and this set never collapses them (SRS 2.3).

6. Flow arrows

One arrow per cell. Direction is flow.dirDeg, length is flow.speedMps. Where flow is null no arrow is drawn at all - a zero-length arrow or a default-direction arrow is never drawn, because either would invent a measurement (FR3.5).

7. Coverage bar - C06, never rendered apart from a risk value

Four segments in the fixed order observed, not-enough-dwell, stale, gap. A risk score with no coverage beside it is not permitted anywhere in this set, because a zone can only look calm by being unobserved (FR4.6).

8. Frame chrome and callouts

Every frame carries a header strip outside the wireframe naming its ID, its design.md ID, its viewport, its role and its state. Numbered blue markers sit on the wireframe and match a numbered note in the right margin, and each note names the requirement it satisfies. A wireframe that cannot name its requirement is decoration.

9. Domain strings - C10 renders these, nothing else does

Exactly the identifiers in srs.md Appendix A. No synonym appears anywhere in this set: cell not tile, zone not area, wind-map overlay not heatmap, Stampede not Crush.
